TERM 1 — Measurement and Geometry
1. Metric Units and Scientific Measurements
Meaning: This topic deals with units used to measure length, mass, capacity, area, and volume, including expressing very large or very small numbers using scientific notation.

Formulas:

1 km = 1,000 m

1 m = 100 cm

1 cm = 10 mm

1 kg = 1,000 g

1 L = 1,000 mL

1 m² = 10,000 cm²

1 m³ = 1,000 L

N = a × 10ⁿ

Where: 1 ≤ |a| < 10 and n is an integer.

2. Unit Conversions
Meaning: Unit conversion is the process of changing a measurement from one unit to another without changing its actual value.

Formulas:

New Value = Given Value × Conversion Factor

Speed = Distance / Time

1 hour = 60 minutes

1 day = 24 hours

1 hectare = 10,000 m²

1 foot = 30.48 cm

3. Surface Area of Three-Dimensional Objects
Meaning: Surface area is the total area covering the outside of a three-dimensional object.

Formulas:

Rectangular Prism: SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)

Cube: SA = 6s²

Cylinder: SA = 2πr² + 2πrh

Curved Surface Area of a Cylinder: CSA = 2πrh

Cone: SA = πr² + πrℓ

Sphere: SA = 4πr²

Regular Pyramid: SA = B + ½Pℓ

General Prism: SA = 2B + Ph

For combined solids, add only the exposed surface areas.

Where: SA = surface area, B = base area, P = base perimeter, r = radius, h = height, ℓ = slant height, l = length, w = width, s = side, and π ≈ 3.1416.

4. Volume of Three-Dimensional Objects
Meaning: Volume is the amount of space occupied by a three-dimensional object.

Formulas:

Cube: V = s³

Rectangular Prism: V = lwh

General Prism: V = Bh

Pyramid: V = ⅓Bh

Cylinder: V = πr²h

Cone: V = ⅓πr²h

Sphere: V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

For combined solids, add or subtract volumes as needed.

5. Scale: Maps, Blueprints, and Sketches
Meaning: Scale represents the relationship between a distance shown in a drawing and the corresponding actual distance.

Formulas:

Scale = Drawing Distance / Actual Distance

Actual Distance = Drawing Distance × Scale Factor

Drawing Distance = Actual Distance / Scale Factor

For a scale of 1:n, multiply the drawing distance by n to get the actual distance.

Always convert both distances to the same unit first.

6. Currency Conversion
Meaning: Currency conversion is the process of changing an amount of money from one currency to another using an exchange rate.

Formulas:

Converted Amount = Original Amount × Exchange Rate

Pesos = US Dollars × Exchange Rate

US Dollars = Pesos / Exchange Rate

7. Time Conversions
Meaning: Time conversion involves changing units of time or calculating the duration between two times.

Formulas:

Elapsed Time = End Time − Start Time

Net Time = Elapsed Time − Break Time

1 hour = 60 minutes

1 minute = 60 seconds

1 day = 24 hours

8. Temperature Conversions
Meaning: Temperature conversion changes a temperature reading from one scale to another.

Formulas:

Celsius to Fahrenheit: F = (9/5)C + 32

Fahrenheit to Celsius: C = (5/9)(F − 32)

Celsius to Kelvin: K = C + 273.15

Kelvin to Celsius: C = K − 273.15

TERM 2 — Measurement and Geometry
1. Trigonometric Ratios in Right Triangles
Meaning: Trigonometric ratios describe the relationship between the sides and angles of a right triangle.

Formulas:

Sine: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse

Cosine: cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

Tangent: tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Pythagorean Theorem: a² + b² = c²

Where c is the hypotenuse.

2. Right and Oblique Triangles
Meaning: Right triangles contain one 90° angle, while oblique triangles have no 90° angle.

Formulas:

Law of Sines: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C

Law of Cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

b² = a² + c² − 2ac cos B

3. Angles of Elevation and Depression
Meaning: The angle of elevation is measured upward from the horizontal, while the angle of depression is measured downward from the horizontal.

Formulas:

tan θ = Vertical Height / Horizontal Distance

h = d tan θ

d = h / tan θ

Total Height = d tan θ + Eye Height

4. Bearings and Navigation
Meaning: Bearings describe direction using angles measured clockwise from north. Navigation problems may involve calculating direction and distance.

Formulas:

Distance = √(x² + y²)

Bearing Angle = tan⁻¹(East Displacement / North Displacement)

Bearings are commonly expressed using three digits, from 000° to 359°.

5. Area of Oblique Triangles
Meaning: The area of an oblique triangle can be determined using its side lengths or two sides and their included angle.

Formulas:

Heron's Formula: A = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]

Semi-perimeter: s = (a + b + c) / 2

Using Two Sides and Included Angle: A = ½ab sin C

A = ½bc sin A

A = ½ac sin B

6. Length and Distance
Meaning: Length measures the size of an object, while distance measures the separation between two points or the path traveled.

Formulas:

Distance at Constant Speed: d = vt

Distance Between Two Points: d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

Rectangle Diagonal: d = √(l² + w²)

7. Area and Perimeter of Residential Spaces
Meaning: Area measures the surface covered by a shape, while perimeter measures the total distance around its boundary.

Formulas:

Square Area: A = s²

Square Perimeter: P = 4s

Rectangle Area: A = lw

Rectangle Perimeter: P = 2(l + w)

Triangle Area: A = ½bh

Triangle Perimeter: P = a + b + c

Circle Area: A = πr²

Circle Circumference: C = 2πr

Fence Length = Perimeter − Gate Width

Total Cost = Fence Length × Cost per Unit

Required Flooring Area = Floor Area × (1 + Extra Rate)

8. Volume and Capacity of Three-Dimensional Objects
Meaning: Volume measures the space occupied by an object, while capacity measures how much a container can hold.

Formulas:

Rectangular Tank: V = lwh

Cube-shaped Container: V = s³

Cylinder: V = πr²h

Prism: V = Bh

1 m³ = 1,000 L

1 L = 1,000 mL

Liquid Volume = Total Capacity × Filled Fraction

Additional Volume = Total Capacity − Current Volume

9. Volume, Capacity, and Cost Estimation
Meaning: Estimation determines the amount of material needed and its approximate cost for projects such as painting walls, flooring rooms, or filling containers.

Formulas:

Material Needed = Total Area or Volume / Coverage per Unit

Paint Needed = (Net Wall Area × Number of Coats) / Coverage per Liter

Wall Area = 2h(l + w)

Net Area = Wall Area − Doors and Windows Area

Total Cost = Quantity Needed × Price per Unit

Round up when purchasing whole units of materials.
